# CuriousNext goal-first walkthrough

## Simple explanation

Tell CuriousNext what you want to learn, choose how you want it explained, and practise with feedback that helps you decide what comes next. You receive a path, learn through examples and visuals, then explain the idea in your own words before choosing your next step.

## Captured-screen walkthrough

The captioned MP4 uses verified fictional browser screenshots, assembled with Swift and AVFoundation. It is a sequence of captured screens, rather than a continuous recording of user interaction. No generated narration is included, because the user's own voice replacement awaits their recording. The scene manifest preserves image names and timing for reproduction with `swift scripts/render-captured-walkthrough.swift docs/design-artifacts/goal-first-tutor --silent` on macOS. The renderer refuses to overwrite its destination.

The fractions story begins with a personal goal and separate explanation, visual and time preferences. The learner receives visual teaching and matching practice, then submits mistaken reasoning about equal shares and denominator size. Specific feedback leads to a fresh corrective task, an additional beginner explanation and successful supported practice. The recommendation advances to the next module, while saved work retains the original mistakes and feedback. Recovery checks preserve the frozen submission and one evidence record after a failed service-port assessment, real provider retry and process restart.

## Presentation claims and limits

- Uniqueness: demonstrate the combination of goal approval, visual teaching, teach-back and inspectable feedback, without claiming this is the first tutoring application.
- Scalability: explain indexed owned persistence, immutable lesson reuse and lightweight native visuals, while acknowledging that geographic scale, localisation and load performance have not been established.
- Codex usage: show the three reviewed development slices, meaningful tests, preserved rejected generations and corrected recovery races, while identifying self-review accurately.
- Presentation: use the fictional screenshots and preserved feedback to tell one coherent learning story, with captions until the user's voice is available.

The four-topic live study uses agent-authored fictional answers and validates bounded integration behaviour. It does not establish general teaching accuracy or learning gains. Generated evidence represents supported practice only, while independent demonstration, retention, actual screen-reader acceptance, provider/curriculum review and deployment remain open. Existing M4 scoring disagreements, M5 and M6 are separate work.
