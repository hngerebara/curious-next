CY-2 · Recognise phishing

Objective

Identify suspicious requests, choose a safe action and explain an independent verification route.

Learn

A phishing message tries to persuade you to disclose information or take an unsafe action by pretending to be trustworthy. Urgency, authority or familiar branding deserve scrutiny, but one cue alone does not prove fraud.

A display name or logo is easy to imitate. A secure connection symbol does not prove legitimacy. Good spelling is not proof of authenticity.

If an unexpected message asks you to sign in, pay or share sensitive information, pause. Verify through a route you already trust, such as opening the official app yourself. Do not use the message’s contact details as your verification route.

Worked example: ParcelPoint

You have not placed an order. A fictional message says: “Your parcel will be returned in 30 minutes. Pay a small redelivery fee at parcelpoint-pay.example.” The destination is inert.

Inspect the requested payment, the unexpected context and the urgency. These support caution. They do not authenticate the sender.

Visual decision path and text equivalent

Unexpected sensitive request → Pause → Verify through a trusted independent route → Decide and report where appropriate.
