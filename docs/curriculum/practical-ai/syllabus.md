# Practical AI syllabus

## Review status

Draft, pending subject review. Publication is gated by the [reference register](../../references/curriculum-sources.md).

## Assessment baseline

Each module has one objective ID, assessed through distinct practical and conceptual criteria. All criteria listed below are required for initial demonstration. Hints make the attempt assisted. Successful supported practice establishes Developing, not Demonstrated. Recall checks retention and never substitutes for initial practical proof. Transfer uses a new context with the same objective.


## Modules


Outcome: complete a small AI-assisted task, check the output, and explain the choices made. AI-generated polish alone is not evidence of learner ability.

| Module / objective | Prerequisite | Learn and practise | Independent application and teach-back criteria | Remediation | Delayed check / transfer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI-1: Understand strengths and limits | None | Compare fluent accurate and inaccurate responses. Classify tasks needing source verification. | Evaluate a supplied response. Identify a useful application and a limitation, distinguish fluency from evidence, and justify a verification step. | Compare one unsupported claim with its source. | Later identify an unsupported claim. Transfer from a study example to a business example. |
| AI-2: Write clear prompts | AI-1 readiness | Decompose task, audience, context, constraints, and output format. Improve a vague instruction. | Write and run a prompt for a new brief. Meet the brief's task, context, constraints, and format criteria, then explain why each relevant element was included. | Repair the specific missing requirement rather than copy a template. | Reconstruct key requirements later. Transfer from a customer FAQ to a study guide. |
| AI-3: Improve through iteration | AI-2 readiness | Inspect an output against requirements and revise deliberately. | Identify two concrete output gaps, revise instructions to address them, compare before/after evidence, and explain residual limitations. | Compare a vague revision with one tied to a specific gap. | Diagnose a fresh output later. Transfer to a different brief and format. |
| AI-4: Verify and protect information | AI-1 readiness | Check against supplied sources and redact unnecessary sensitive details. | Trace claims to evidence, correct unsupported statements, avoid unnecessary disclosure, and justify verification and privacy decisions. | Work through one claim-source pair or redaction decision. | Recheck a new claim later. Transfer between a workplace and study context. |
| AI-5: Complete a workflow | AI-2, AI-3, AI-4 readiness | Guided brief-to-output workflow with checkpoints. | Produce a usable result, preserve prompt/revision history, substantiate claims, handle privacy, and defend the process. Required criteria cover all preceding skills. | Address failed criteria with targeted tasks before a fresh final attempt. | Later explain a consequential decision. Transfer to a second workflow brief. |

Readiness means demonstrated prerequisite evidence or provisional diagnostic placement. Provisional placement permits learning in later modules without marking prerequisites demonstrated. Independent integrated proof requires the prerequisite evidence or explicit coverage of those criteria within the same assessment. Skipping placement starts at AI-1.

AI-5 offers three comparable fictional briefs: a customer FAQ, a source-based study guide, or a requirements-based proposal. Each supplies a bounded reference pack and uses the same required workflow criteria. Examples align with the learner's goal. Do not infer factual correctness from model agreement.
